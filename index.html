Estudios de Mercado I · Tema 3 — In-depth interview worksheet

What you do: run 3–5 short in-depth interviews on ONE topic — one respondent, one interviewer, a semi-structured guide — record them, get them transcribed, then apply thematic analysis across the interviews and laddering (attribute → consequence → value) to reach a decision for the client.

The topic — the student night out. Client: a new events / nightlife app that wants to win the student segment. Research question: how do students decide where to go out — music, venue, people, budget — and what makes a night feel “worth it”?
▸ Change the topic (keep it the same as your focus group if you will triangulate)
Interviewer's guide — a funnel, ~15–20 min per interview (3.2). Semi-structured: cover the topics, but follow whatever is interesting.
  1. Warm-up (2m): “Tell me a bit about a normal week for you.” (no wrong answers)
  2. Broad context (4m): “Walk me through the last time you went out — from deciding to getting home.” Let them frame it in their words.
  3. Core (6m): “How did you choose where to go? What made it worth it — or not? If you had to give up the music, the place, the people or the price, which goes first?”
  4. Deep probes / ladder (5m): take two or three things they named and climb: “why does that matter to you?” → “and what does that give you?” → until you reach a value. Ask for a critical incident: “tell me about a night that went wrong”.
  5. Close (1m): “What haven't I asked that matters?”
The three habits that ruin an interview: asking two questions at once · filling the silence (count to three) · accepting the first answer.
Who: choose on purpose (purposive) — e.g. someone who goes out every week, someone who rarely does, someone new to the city. Stop adding interviews when the last one tells you nothing new (saturation).
